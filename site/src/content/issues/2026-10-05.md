  summary: "The Dual-LLM pattern claims formal injection safety by fixing execution plans before touching untrusted data. This paper shows the guarantee breaks for computer-use agents, because GUI plans must branch on anticipated runtime content. That opens branch steering: an adversary crafts page content to coerce the agent down a hazardous but pre-approved path with no explicit instruction. STEER-Bench (101 tasks, 9 domains) shows 94.4% success against standard CUAs, 89.5% against vanilla Dual-LLM. COBRA pairs trusted branching plans with ahead-of-time capability constraints, dropping success to 0% at 97% utility."
  takeaway: "If you rely on Dual-LLM isolation for a CUA, test whether your plans branch on untrusted content — that's your exposure, and plan-time capability bounding is the fix."

  summary: "Action speculation drafts an agent's next action for the target model to verify, mirroring speculative decoding. Prior work used large off-the-shelf drafters (accurate but slow) or small ones (fast but rarely agreeing). This paper builds a latency framework weighing a round's gain — drafter accuracy times remaining steps — against drafting, verification, and tool-execution cost. LEAP keeps the drafter at 0.6B but trains it on the target's action sequences, so it agrees often, yielding up to 60% end-to-end wall-clock speedup with no systematic change in task success."

  summary: "Teams pick prompt-injection detectors by their public benchmark scores. This work shows those scores don't predict in-agent behavior. By replaying ground-truth tool calls from AgentDojo and tau-bench without an LLM, then labeling injected outputs by differential replay, it evaluates fifteen detectors plus Prompt Guard 2.
