  summary: "A clean negative result on scaling automated research: iterating longer, running agents in parallel, and letting them communicate all plateau as agents accumulate history and anchor to their own past attempts, making smaller edits. The fix is to forget — periodically discard search state and every session's candidate history, restart from the best candidate found so far. On four tasks spanning heuristic programming, kernel optimization, and model training, forgetting yields up to 15% over parallel agents and 39% over a single agent on the same budget."
  takeaway: "For open-ended search with iterative agents, periodic state resets from the best candidate may buy more than additional compute — what the agent remembers is a tunable knob."
