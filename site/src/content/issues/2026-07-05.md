  summary: "The common assumption is that an ambiguous prompt makes an LLM incoherent—asked repeatedly, it produces semantically distinct implementations reflecting the ambiguity. Richter and Papadakis show the opposite: models frequently collapse onto a single incorrect interpretation and generate it consistently. They call this detrimental semantic collapse, and it affects over 10% of MBPP, 3% of HumanEval, and 32% of LiveCodeBench tasks—all assumed well-specified. Deliberately injecting underspecification raises the rate more than fivefold, breaking disambiguation techniques that use incoherence as a proxy for ambiguity."
