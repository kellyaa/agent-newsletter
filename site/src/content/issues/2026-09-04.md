  summary: "Multi-turn agentic RL treats credit assignment as targeting — localizing credit onto the turns that mattered. This paper argues targeting is second-order and coverage is what matters, formalized via verifier information density V_d. Terminal-state verifiers sit deep in low-V_d territory (k=1 in 98% of rollouts, while success needs 5-8 prerequisite steps). On tau²-bench, a uniform dense reward beats sparse binary outcome, while concentrating the same advantage on progress turns or random turns is equally harmful. Pre-registered replications across model families. Genuinely surprising, carefully controlled."

  author: "Marc Bara"

  summary: "Spawning more agents doesn't add evidence — it can multiply the same evidence while looking like corroboration. The paper formalizes this as an epistemic Sybil problem: a report adds nothing when its mutual information with existing reports, given the parameter, is zero, and no report-only aggregator can generally tell replication from independent corroboration. Across 20,000+ controlled calls, holding one evidence root fixed while report count rises from 1 to 32 collapses posterior coverage from 0.940 to 0.263. A correlated-extraction aggregator restores calibration. Rigorous and practically important."
  takeaway: "If your multi-agent system aggregates reports, weight by independent evidence roots — not by how many agents agreed."
