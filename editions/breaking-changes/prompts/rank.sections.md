- **Papers.** Novelty and depth lead. Be tough on surveys, toy benchmarks and "we tried prompting X on a code task" pieces. Demand a real study: field data from real teams or repos, a controlled comparison, a measured effect with its confound named. A paper that is merely on-topic, or that measures on a benchmark far from how teams actually work, lands in the 3–5 range, not 7.
- **News.** Relevance and credibility lead. Concrete (version numbers, deprecation dates, changed defaults, named incidents, CVEs, pricing or policy changes that affect a team) beats vague. A "we launched X" with no engineering substance caps at 5. A release or incident that changes how a team should work can hit 7–8.
- **Blogs.** Depth and novelty lead. A practitioner's earned account of what happened when their team adopted, restricted or measured AI tooling can hit 7+ without academic rigor. Listicles, "10 prompts for developers," "AI will replace developers" takes and vendor marketing cap at 4. Postmortems, measured before/after reports and honest failure stories → high.
