  summary: "A sharp negative result on agent memory: storing inferred abstractions — rules, summaries, skills — not just raw trajectories lets errors propagate. A faulty abstraction shapes execution, those trajectories become the basis for new abstractions that regenerate the same error, a loop the authors call error reproduction that outlives the original faulty entry. Their Anti-Persistence design keeps only trajectories and outcomes, synthesizing task guidance on demand and discarding it after use. Across benchmarks and backbones it wins held-out success at the lowest average LLM-call cost."
  takeaway: "If your agent caches derived strategies, audit whether it's recycling its own past mistakes before trusting the memory store."
