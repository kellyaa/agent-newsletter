  title: "Bursts That Outlast Themselves: Persistent Degradation in Prefix-Cached Agent Serving"
  author: "Wen Huang, Jack Liu"
  score: 8
  tags:
  - "infra"
  - "cost-latency"
  - "observability"
  summary: "A clean demonstration that prefix-cached agent serving is metastable. On vLLM with an identical base workload, a few minutes of extra arrivals leaves the server stuck in a degraded regime — ~10% hit ratio, half throughput, a backlog that keeps growing for 70 minutes — while its no-burst twin stays healthy in 9 of 12 pairs and never the reverse. The collapse vanishes without prefix caching and reproduces on Qwen3-32B/SWE-bench shapes and SGLang. Recovery is the hard part: throttling admissions fixed 1 of 7 runs, suspending sessions fixed all; a KV-demand budgeter prevented it entirely with no per-workload tuning."
  takeaway: "If you serve agents behind prefix caching, admission throttling won't drain a collapse — suspending admitted sessions or bounding resident KV demand is what recovers."
