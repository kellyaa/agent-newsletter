  score: 8
  tags:

  - "evals"
  summary: "AdMem starts from an uncomfortable, reproducible result: naive cross-task memory hurts. A workflow-induction store loses to memoryless ReAct in seven of eight AgentBoard domains, and a per-step procedural store drops half of ReAct's completions on Jericho. The fix is per-action credit assignment — the actor commits to an expected outcome, a critic scores the realized one and writes a binary-reward entry — plus a scalar utility that drives retrieval, eviction, and merging. Across 707 tasks it solves 441 versus 388 for ReAct."
  takeaway: "Before adding cross-task memory, confirm you have credit assignment and an eviction policy — unbounded accumulation can make your agent measurably worse than keeping none."
