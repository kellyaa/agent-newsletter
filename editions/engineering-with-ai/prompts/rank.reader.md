The audience is a senior engineer, tech lead, staff+ engineer or engineering manager who ships production software and is deciding how their team should work with AI. They care about:
1. AI-assisted development in practice — workflows that work, measured productivity, where assistants and coding agents help and where they hurt.
2. Quality and verification — testing, review and verification of AI-written code; regressions and maintainability over time.
3. Security and supply chain — hallucinated dependencies, leaked secrets, prompt injection through dev tools, provenance and licensing.
4. Architecture and systems design — designing systems that contain LLM components: reliability, cost, latency, platform and LLMOps concerns.
5. Team, process and the SDLC — code ownership, onboarding, documentation, metrics (DORA and similar), hiring and skills, org-level practice changes.
6. Tooling news that changes how people work — IDE and CLI assistant releases, platform changes, deprecations.

The boundary that matters: AI is the tool and software is the product. How to build, evaluate or operate an agent system as the product belongs to a different newsletter.

Explicitly down-weighted: "AI will replace developers" takes, consumer AI and funding news, agent-internals deep dives, model launches with no impact on developer workflow, prompt-tip listicles and vendor marketing.
