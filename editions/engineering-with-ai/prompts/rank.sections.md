- **Papers.** Novelty and depth lead. Be tough on surveys, toy benchmarks and "we tried prompting X on a code task" pieces. Demand a real study: field data from real teams or repos, a controlled comparison, a measured effect with its confound named. A paper that is merely on-topic, or that measures on a benchmark far from how teams actually work, lands in the 3–5 range, not 7.
- **News.** Relevance and credibility lead. Concrete (version numbers, deprecation dates, changed defaults, named incidents, CVEs, pricing or policy changes that affect a team) beats vague. A "we launched X" with no engineering substance caps at 5. A release or incident that changes how a team should work can hit 7–8. General AI news with no consequence for how software gets built (jobs, regulation, lawsuits, funding, model launches) caps at 3, however many HN points it has. Ask HN threads and self posts where engineers compare their own workflows count as first-hand practice; score them on the question and any text, as a blog post would be.
- **Blogs.** First-hand evidence leads, then novelty. Score 7+ only for a first-hand account (the author's own team, codebase or experiment) with at least two of: concrete artifacts or configuration, numbers or a before/after, honest failure modes, a technique a well-read practitioner hasn't seen yet. A thoughtful essay or opinion piece with no first-hand detail caps at 5. A company engineering post about how it uses AI internally is first-hand; a vendor post about its own product is marketing and caps at 4. Conference talks arrive as video descriptions; score what the description shows, and cap a sponsor talk pitching the speaker's product at 4. Listicles, "10 prompts for developers," "AI will replace developers" takes and roundups of other people's posts cap at 4. Many candidates arrive with only a short RSS teaser. Don't penalize the teaser for being short; when the title and teaser clearly signal a first-hand account ("how we", "what I learned", "a year of", numbers, named tools), score on that signal.
- **The `why` field.** Start it with "first-hand:" or "second-hand:" so the operator can audit how the item was judged.
